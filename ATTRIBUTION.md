## Wikipedia, *Signs of AI writing*

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing

Maintained by WikiProject AI Cleanup. **Licensed CC BY-SA 4.0**, which is why this repository carries the same licence.

The throat-clearing openers, sentence templates, grand-finale closers, transitions and hedges, vocabulary, and format-and-mechanics families all draw on that page's observations. Its patterns come from cleaning up thousands of instances of AI-generated text on Wikipedia, which is a body of evidence nothing else here comes close to.

The framing of individual patterns has been reorganised and rewritten, and the emphasis differs, but the underlying observations are theirs.

## What is original

The **meta-commentary family** and the **ban-the-construction rule**.

Neither appears in any list above, and the reason is structural rather than an oversight: every published list is built from encyclopedic or marketing prose, and neither of those genres has a narrator talking to a reader. The pattern only becomes visible when you are reading an assistant's output every day.

It was found that way. A user reading his assistant's writing named "said plainly" and "worth noting" as tells. The vault was swept clean of those strings, and the next message contained "two things worth you knowing", which is what produced the rule about banning the shape rather than the instances.

The action-versus-information distinction, which stops the rule from stripping genuine judgement out of writing, came from applying it at scale and finding that 66 of 83 hits were not tells at all.
